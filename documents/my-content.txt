The Lord of the Rings by J.R.R. Tolkien is an epic high-fantasy trilogy consisting of The Fellowship of the Ring, The Two Towers, and The Return of the King.

**1. The Fellowship of the Ring**
Frodo Baggins, a hobbit of the Shire, inherits the One Ring from his uncle Bilbo and learns it must be destroyed to stop the dark lord Sauron from conquering Middle-earth. Guided by the wizard Gandalf, Frodo sets out with eight companions—forming the Fellowship—to journey to Mount Doom in Mordor. Along the way, they face treacherous terrain, enemies, and inner conflicts. The Fellowship is eventually broken, and Frodo continues the mission with only his loyal friend Sam.

**2. The Two Towers**
The story splits into parallel narratives: Frodo and Sam continue their journey toward Mordor, guided by the tormented creature Gollum, who once possessed the Ring. Meanwhile, Aragorn, Legolas, and Gimli pursue the kidnapped hobbits Merry and Pippin, who escape captivity and encounter the Ents—ancient tree-beings. Gandalf returns, reborn as Gandalf the White, and helps rally forces to fight against Sauron’s ally Saruman. The war for Middle-earth begins as darkness spreads.

**3. The Return of the King**
As Sauron's armies launch a final assault, the forces of men, led by Aragorn, Gandalf, and others, prepare for a last stand. Aragorn claims his rightful place as king and leads a desperate charge to distract Sauron. Meanwhile, Frodo and Sam struggle through Mordor, nearing Mount Doom. After immense hardship and a final betrayal by Gollum, the Ring is destroyed—accidentally—ending Sauron’s reign. Peace is restored, Aragorn is crowned, and the hobbits return home changed. Frodo eventually sails west, seeking healing beyond Middle-earth.
